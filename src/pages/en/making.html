FROM WOOD TO SOUND

How bagpipes are made

Making a single instrument is a long journey. Each bagpipe takes dozens of hours of handwork before it sounds for the first time. Below, I will guide you through the nine crafts involved.

From wood to sound

1

Woodturner

The process begins by turning the wooden parts of the instrument. Each instrument has nine turned pieces, made from the finest maple. The most demanding part is the melody pipe, with a tuning screw for every note; it requires a combination of turning and careful hand-finishing.

2

Carpenter

I shape, sand, stain and varnish the bellows boards. I hand-paint folk patterns on every set of bellows.

3

Shoemaker

The bellows must be sealed carefully: covered in leather, glued and secured with brass tacks.

4

Carver

My bagpipes feature a turned head in the shape of a goat or a devil.

5

Leatherworker

The bag is sewn from goatskin and lined with an airtight membrane, so it holds air well and is easy to play.

6

Brassworker

The brass parts are carefully finished and polished. Bent brass elbows connect the pipes to the cow horns, giving the instrument a warm, resonant tone.

7

Working with horn

Cow horns are boiled, cleared of their core, then ground and machine-polished to a flawless finish.

8

Bagpipe maker

The instrument must be designed correctly and then assembled.

9

Reed maker and tuner

Making the reeds and tuning the instrument is the most specialised part of the work. The reed has a decisive effect on volume, tone colour, how hard the instrument feels to play and its tuning. Players have very different preferences, so when I know them in advance, I do my best to accommodate every customer.
